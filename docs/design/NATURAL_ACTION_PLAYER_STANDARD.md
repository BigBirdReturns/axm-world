# Natural action player standard

Status: post-v1 player-product law for deterministic action arcs.

## Controlling failure

The first continuous UNDERDRAIN pilot proved authored identity, exact Arc replay, mechanism semantics, persistent consequence, and a playable successor. Its player representation still behaved like an inspection surface. The browser surrounded a small abstract simulation with panels, badges, records, and explanatory copy. The Unity camera framed the complete actor set, the fallback bodies were primitives, and desktop input exposed world-axis movement plus test-oriented key bindings.

Those properties make a useful conformance demonstrator. They do not make the frog-brawler-shaped game that motivated the action runtime.

## Player-facing target

A qualifying desktop action arc begins with an ordinary game control surface:

```text
free camera yaw and pitch
player-follow shoulder framing
camera-relative movement
continuous aim reduced to the accepted deterministic trace
buffered light and heavy attacks
buffered dodge and parry
held mechanism work
readable enemy anticipation, contact, and recovery
short contact holds and camera feedback
minimal health, objective, and interaction HUD
```

The player should be able to enter motion, turn the camera, attack, dodge, parry, and work a mechanism without reading an authority term or opening a record panel.

## Default controls

```text
WASD / left stick       move relative to the camera
mouse / right stick     free look
left mouse / face 0     horizontal light sweep
right mouse / face 2    committed heavy crush
Space or Shift / face 1 dodge
Q or L / left bumper    parry
E or F / face 3         hold or tap mechanism work
Escape                  release cursor
```

Input buffering is an ingress rule. It may retain a short edge until the next legal idle tick, but it cannot change attack timing, damage, collision, objective law, or outcome. Held mechanism work remains visible on every sampled tick.

## Camera law

Player follow is the default. The camera follows the player, accepts free yaw and pitch, and gently recenters behind actual player motion only after a period without manual look. It may add presentation impulses and field-of-view response. It may not select targets, change action state, or frame the full encounter by default.

Actor-set framing remains available only as an explicit diagnostic mode for conformance inspection and capture.

## Contact and readability

A hit, parry, or player impact may request a short real-time hold. While held, the runtime advances no state and emits no trace row. The subsequent trace therefore remains the exact sequence Arc replays and accepts.

Enemy anticipation, active contact, recovery, stagger, and defeat must be visible from body motion and local feedback. A dashboard label is not a substitute for an attack telegraph.

## HUD and records

The live HUD is limited to player health, current authored verb, progress, nearby mechanism work, and a temporary controls reminder. Receipts, digests, authority ledgers, and structural evidence remain export, pause, or debug surfaces. They may never compete with the advancing action view.

## Representation ladder

```text
source and conformance fixture
  -> deterministic action mirror
  -> natural desktop player plane
  -> authored bodies, arena, motion, effects, and audio
  -> Windows player session
  -> XR ingress and physical safety
  -> exact Arc replay and accepted consequence
```

A browser or primitive fixture may prove law and reachability. It does not satisfy the natural desktop player plane merely because the same action state can be completed.

## Acceptance boundary

Source and hosted conformance can prove camera mode, control mapping, buffering, held mechanism input, trace-safe contact holds, minimal HUD composition, deterministic replay, and absence of hidden state mutation. Actual feel still requires a real Unity player session with mouse and keyboard and a controller. Unity import, frame behavior, animation quality, collision-free camera behavior, Windows player operation, Quest operation, and human preference remain their own receipts.

## Control question

Can a cold player move and look immediately, express attack direction through the camera, buffer a defensive or offensive intent during recovery, read the threat from the body rather than a panel, perform the authored mechanism under pressure, and finish the encounter without needing to know that a receipt system exists?
