# The one-a.m. player standard

Status: post-v1 product law for authored player experiences.

## Why this exists

The "one-a.m. player" is a cold player who opens the game tired, alone, without source access, without a designer beside them, and without patience for an architecture demonstration. They skim. They press the most obvious button. They may miss one prompt, choose an imperfect action, stop mid-beat, reload, or lose the first exchange.

That behavior is not misuse. It is the controlling product environment.

This standard reconstructs the durable acceptance law previously proven by the Program 001 / First Charter cold runs:

- one directed chain rather than competing product surfaces;
- authored character, conflict, choice, consequence, and next action as one scene;
- anticipation, commit, impact, consequence, and aftermath as distinct beats;
- a static final frame that answers what changed, who changed, what was recorded, and what happens next;
- a zero-explanation blind run in which the operator independently describes the player role, conflict, choice, consequence, and next action;
- exact persistence and resume through the same cartridge identity.

Automated completion is necessary and insufficient. A scripted browser can prove reachability, determinism, persistence, accessibility, and authority boundaries. It cannot prove that a cold player understood why they acted or what their action changed.

## The five questions

After the first authored consequence, a player who received no explanation must be able to answer, in their own words:

1. **Who am I here?** The player role, not the engine or receiver architecture.
2. **What problem am I trying to solve?** The immediate conflict and ordinary stake.
3. **What did I choose?** A meaningful authored commitment, not merely a control binding.
4. **What changed because of it?** The accepted consequence in the world and among its people.
5. **What do I do next?** One concrete, playable next action or inherited obligation.

The receipt records normalized expected and observed identifiers. A receipt cannot pass because an author copied the expected answers into the observation fields.

## Directed first-session chain

A qualifying first session must present this causal chain:

```text
inhabited situation
  -> legible player role and ordinary stake
  -> one authored commitment
  -> action whose verbs express that commitment
  -> discovery or pressure revealed during play
  -> accepted consequence
  -> visible persistent world change
  -> durable record
  -> one concrete next playable action
```

The exact presentation may vary. The causal roles may not be omitted or moved entirely into optional lore and post-hoc prose.

## Hard acceptance rules

### Cold entry

- Fresh storage or a new profile.
- No source code, authoring packet, test fixture, walkthrough, or verbal explanation.
- The primary title or cartridge action enters the authored situation, not a generic mechanic disconnected from it.
- Technical authority terms may exist in inspectable custody surfaces, but not as required comprehension on the primary player path.
- Tutorial content occupies no space needed by the current decision and backgrounds itself when the player acts.

### First meaningful action

- One obvious advancing action per beat. A bounded defer action such as `Not yet` is permitted.
- No alternate quick-resolve, shell, map, debug, or advanced action may compete with the authored advance.
- The first meaningful authored decision must be reachable within 60 seconds on the reference device without reading optional documentation.
- A control tutorial does not count as the authored decision.

### Story and action coupling

- The action objective must describe the verb the player actually performs. A label such as `Repair the sluice` fails when the only completion predicate is an unrelated enemy-defeat count.
- The player's authored commitment must change at least one runtime information, relationship, affordance, route, presentation, or consequence surface. It need not change deterministic difficulty.
- At least one important story fact must be learned through action, actor behavior, or an in-play consequence. A reveal shown only on the result screen fails.
- Characters must act through their authored methods during the playable sequence; storing their methods in authoring data alone is insufficient.

### Consequence and continuity

- The result is accepted by the owning authority before it changes campaign state.
- The accepted result produces a visible world delta, not only a receipt paragraph.
- The changed place, relationship, obligation, resource, or institution must be inspectable after leaving the encounter.
- A durable record survives reload under the same authored identity.
- The next obligation must be a concrete playable action, not a teaser for unimplemented content.
- Resume restores the exact incomplete beat or completed consequence without replaying session-only staging.

### Recovery and one-a.m. behavior

- Missing one tutorial prompt cannot hard-lock the experience.
- The first ordinary mistake or loss cannot discard all authored context and return the player to a blank title.
- Retry preserves completed story beats and states what remains to be done.
- The experience waits for the player; it does not punish absence or require real-time return.

### Device dignity

- Desktop and mobile preserve the same decisions, causal order, and consequence.
- Touch targets and text remain operable at the declared minimum viewport.
- Reduced motion collapses time, not information.
- Muted play loses no required story or action information.

## Evidence tiers

### Structural gate

Machine-verifiable evidence:

- exact candidate identity;
- one advancing action per beat;
- authored decision before action commitment;
- objective labels bound to actual player verbs;
- in-play reveal marker;
- accepted-authority result;
- persistent world delta;
- durable record and exact resume;
- implemented next playable action;
- desktop/mobile parity;
- recovery behavior;
- no required network dependency.

### Blind-player receipt

Observational evidence from a player who did not author or inspect the candidate:

- no assistance events;
- no source or walkthrough knowledge;
- elapsed time to the first authored decision and first consequence;
- observed answers to the five questions, normalized by a separate adjudicator;
- points of hesitation, wrong turns, death, retry, and abandonment;
- whether the player voluntarily continued after the first consequence.

A developer, scripted persona, or model that was given the expected answers may produce diagnostic evidence but not the blind-player receipt.

## Refusal examples

The standard must refuse a candidate that:

- makes the premise optional while the primary `Play` action jumps directly into combat;
- teaches controls safely but never explains why the player is fighting;
- stores a complete story rail in JSON while rendering only a kill wave;
- presents plumbing objectives whose actual predicates are enemy defeats;
- announces the causal reveal only after combat;
- remembers a choice only as result-screen copy;
- emits `campaignEffect: null` and then claims an evolving campaign;
- names a next episode that cannot be entered;
- passes deterministic bot personas but has no zero-explanation blind-player evidence.

## Relationship to existing gates

- Program-of-record identity tests prove authored identity, schema, and registered runtime surfaces. They do not prove player comprehension.
- Gate 7 proves a neutral cartridge can traverse the receiver, complete, export, and resume. It does not prove an authored episode.
- Action parity proves deterministic law across Arc and World/Unity. It does not prove that the player's verb expresses the story objective.
- Narrative succession proves structural story law. It does not prove that the law was staged in the player's session.
- This standard is the conjunctive authored-experience gate over those authorities.

## Release language

A candidate may be called an **action receiver prototype** when deterministic action and provisional World execution work but accepted consequence and continuing world do not.

A candidate may be called a **playable authored episode** only when both the structural gate and a valid blind-player receipt pass on the same candidate identity.
