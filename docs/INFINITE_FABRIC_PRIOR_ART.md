# AXM Infinite Fabric product lineage

The Infinite Fabric program recovers a product direction that predates the current prompt-to-world demonstrations.

## Original product statement

```text
AXM-ARC → PLAYABLE WORLDS

Author once. Play anywhere. Manage like a sim. Play like Nintendo + Roblox.
```

The product roles were already separated:

```text
AUTHOR       AXM-ARC
CARTRIDGE    PACKAGE
ENGINE       AXM-WORLD
PLANET       3D RUNTIME
PLAY         NINTENDO + ROBLOX
```

The governing statement was already:

```text
The cartridge is the source of truth.
The engine is the simulator.
The world is the playground.
```

The runtime output already named Board Mode, Planet Mode, Co-op Play, and UGC/Share. The end-to-end player loop already named Choose Contract, Build Party, Play Encounter, Record Outcome, World Evolves, and New Stories.

## Original Tiny World and BlockQuest direction

The First Charter mockups treated Board, Map, Planet, and later Play as views of one cartridge. The BlockQuest Builder treatment explicitly described a Roblox-ready voxel world whose playable loop connected:

```text
judgment layer
party cost
world reaction
memory ledger
```

Its visible product language was:

```text
BUILD • BATTLE • DECIDE • REMEMBER
```

The world-reaction panels already showed persistent town and route changes, new vendors, repaired roads, returning children, key memories, rewards, and follow-on opportunities. This is the product basis for the current structured world, patch, schema, and ledger contracts.

## Current recovery

QuickPlay supplies fast prompt-to-play incubation. AXM Infinite Fabric supplies canonical persistent world growth. The First Charter Tiny World is the first world of record. Models propose structured patches. Rodoh previews and accepts them. AXM-WORLD renders them. AXM-ARC remains outcome authority where the world is ARC-bound. The memory ledger preserves the consequences.

This repository record establishes product lineage and architecture. It does not assert that a specific third party accessed or copied the work.
